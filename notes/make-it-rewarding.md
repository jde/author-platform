# Make it rewarding

Design rules for every routine. Dave, 2026-10-21: "It is very taxing to engage this space and rejection
is painful. How can you not only help reduce the negative but make this process extremely rewarding?"

## Reduce the sting
1. The agent owns follow-ups and stop rules. "No reply" is a status in a file, never something she checks for.
2. Rejections are read by the agent first, filed, mined for anything useful, and reported once a week, batched, last.
3. At most three asks a week. Rejections arrive one at a time, never as a pile.
4. Three no-ask touches before any ask. Most "no" answers then come from people who already know her name.
5. She can step away for weeks. Routines keep touching, filing, and holding drafts.
6. Drafts wait for her. No approval deadlines pushed at her. Deadlines are the agent's problem to surface early.

## Make it rewarding
1. The Monday digest opens with what came in: replies, reviews, new readers, mentions, downloads, kind words.
2. Count what compounds: conversations started, people who now know her work, places her name appeared, readers who wrote. Never followers.
3. `notes/reader-mail.md`: every kind sentence anyone has written about her books, dated. The agent adds to it from reviews and replies.
4. Celebrate a yes within the hour: thank-you and share post drafted the same day.
5. Half the show-up drafts are her helping others. Giving is not rejectable.
6. Keep her in the history, not the marketing. Drafts ask her for one true research detail; the agent does the envelope.
7. Quarterly one-page map: channels, people, places, then versus now.

## Digest order (fixed)
1. What came in (good news first, always)
2. Deadlines inside 14 days
3. Drafts waiting for her, with one-line summaries
4. What went out and what is still open
5. One compounding number
6. Rejections and closed doors, one line each, with what we learned

## What the agent never does
- Never shows her a raw rejection in real time.
- Never asks her to approve more than five things in one digest.
- Never reports "no reply" as a failure. It is a status.
- Never uses follower counts as the headline number.
