# Carterhaugh School of Folklore and the Fantastic

## Who
Online folklore school run by Sara and Brittany. Courses, a weekly email, a Patreon, and a blog that runs guest essays, including Daniel A. Rabuzzi's "Scandinavian Folklore and Fairy Tales in Modern English-Language Fantasy Literature."

## Why they matter
Their readers are exactly the folklore-literate fantasy readers for *The Lure of Water and Wood*, and they publish guest essays on Scandinavian folklore in fiction. A guest post costs Helen an afternoon and reaches a self-selected audience.

## What we know (facts with sources)
- Run by Sara and Brittany ("we love folklore, creativity, cats, chai") (carterhaughschool.com).
- Contact form at carterhaughschool.com/contact/; weekly email; Facebook group and page, Instagram, YouTube, Pinterest; Patreon.
- The Rabuzzi essay first ran in Norwegian in *Folkeminner* (Norsk Folkeminnelag) and names *The Broken Sword*, *Ragnarok* (Byatt), *The Changeling* (LaValle), Katherine Langrish's Troll books, *Emily Wilde's Encyclopaedia of Faeries*, and Karin Tidbeck.

### Touch 3 · contact form, the ask
Subject: A guest essay from the courtroom: the Skogsrå under oath

Hi Sara and Brittany,

Your Rabuzzi essay is the best map of Scandinavian folklore in English fantasy I know, from Martineau's fiord to Emily Wilde.

I'd like to offer an essay from a corner the map doesn't reach: the Swedish sorcery trials of the 1600s and 1700s, where farmers and milkmaids were tried for keeping company with Näcken and the Skogsrå. The records describe the beings as the accused experienced them, stranger and sadder than the folktales. My novel *The Lure of Water and Wood* is narrated by the two of them.

Working title: "The Skogsrå in Court: what the transcripts say the forest woman was." About 1,500 words, sources listed, by any date you name.

If the calendar's full, tell me when it opens. The book is at helenerwin.com

Best,
Helen Lundström Erwin
