# Talk and podcast topics

Each topic has a plain title and a two-sentence description. Choose the one that fits the audience.

1. **Sweden's sorcery trials, told by the folklore spirits.** Swedish courts in the 1600s and 1700s tried people for relationships with Näcken and the Skogsrå. Helen discusses the trial records and why she chose the spirits as narrators.
2. **The Mora witch trial of 1669 and Salem.** Accounts of the Mora trial were printed across Europe and were still cited when the Salem trials began in 1692. Most English-speaking audiences don't know the Swedish case.
3. **Writing the Skogsrå as a character.** In folklore the Skogsrå is a warning figure. Helen talks about writing her as a member of the village community.
4. **Swedish women's suffrage and café culture.** How Swedish suffragists organized around 1900, and the real cafés where they met.
5. **Elise Ottesen-Jensen.** The Swedish sex-education pioneer who founded RFSU in 1933 and co-founded the International Planned Parenthood Federation. [Helen: add her origin in one phrase]
6. **Building a novel's setting in virtual reality.** How Helen and Christi Fenison built The Foremothers Café, what it cost, and who uses it.
7. **Museums that exist only in virtual reality.** The Foremothers Café as an example of a cultural institution with no physical building.
8. **How research changed a plot.** Virginia's slave laws changed the plot of *James' Journey* while Helen was writing it.
9. **Writing fiction from family history.** Using research into relatives in Sweden and the United States as the basis for novels.
10. **The Swedish folklore calendar.** Easter witches, Walpurgis Night, and Midsummer, and the folklore attached to each.
11. **Writing from trial records.** How to write historical fiction when the main sources are court transcripts and confessions given under pressure.
12. **Self-publishing a 750-page novel.** Length, IngramSpark, and the decision not to cut it. [Helen: only if she wants this on the list.]
