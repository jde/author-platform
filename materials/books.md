# Books

## The Lure of Water and Wood (2023, 752 pages, Powersimple LLC, ISBN 978-0-9862666-6-9)
One line: Sweden's water spirit and forest spirit tell the story of the sorcery trials from the inside, across two centuries of the people accused of loving them.
Paragraph: Näcken lures and drowns. The Skogsrå hides a hollow back and draws men into the woods. Both pass for human. From the 1600s into the 1700s, as Swedish courts try farmers, milkmaids and soldiers for consorting with them, the two beings move through the villages where the accusations start. It is a historical fantasy built on real trial records, and it is told by the creatures the records were about.
Hooks: Easter witches and Blåkulla; Walpurgis; midsummer; October; the Mora trials of 1669; Dee Chainey's October 2025 review ("A truly beautiful book, exploring Sweden's folklore and the beings who inhabit it").
Comparable: The Mercies (Vardø trials), Burial Rites, The Witch of Eye, Emily Wilde's Encyclopaedia of Faeries.
Praise on file: American Swedish Historical Museum (quoted on helenerwin.com) on crafting historical fact with fantasy storytelling. [Helen: exact quote and name.]

## Sour Milk in Sheep's Wool (2021, 342 pages, Powersimple LLC, ISBN 978-0-9862666-4-5)
One line: In Lund around 1900, an unwed mother who moves town with every pregnancy and a café owner who hosts the suffragists after hours find their lives braided together.
Paragraph: Anette says she is a widow. She is not; she has several children by different men and leaves each town before it finds out. Hanna runs a café by day and, after closing, the meeting place where the women who will win the vote argue about how. Elise Ottesen-Jensen, who would go on to found RFSU and co-found the International Planned Parenthood Federation, passes through. It is a novel about reproductive justice before the phrase existed, drawn from the author's own family history.
Hooks: Foremothers Day (Nov 30); Women's History Month; Elise Ottesen-Jensen's birthday (Jan 2); Swedish suffrage anniversaries (May 24, Sep 12); the café in VR.
Comparable: The Women's March (Chiaverini), The Seer (Levitt), The Stockholm Octavo.
Praise on file: "A Moving and Incisive Historical Novel" (Fandango 4 Art House); History Enthusiasts Pick of the Month, Sept 2021.

## James' Journey (2014, 403 pages, Powersimple LLC)
One line: A Virginia planter's son goes north to study, comes home an abolitionist, and runs the Underground Railroad across his own family's land.
Paragraph: Raised to believe what his family believed, James goes to New York and unlearns it. Back in Virginia, with Katherine, and with Ellen and Pap, who are not yet free, he turns the plantation into a station while the neighbors watch. The novel grew out of what the author learned about her husband's relatives, and the Virginia slave laws she researched changed its plot as she wrote.
Hooks: Black History Month; Juneteenth; Quaker and Underground Railroad sites in New Jersey and Pennsylvania.
Praise on file: Bibliophile's Reverie blog tour and review, April 2015; History Enthusiasts Pick of the Month, April 2022.
